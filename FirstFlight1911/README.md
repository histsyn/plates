# FirstFlight1911 — plates

Four document plates for the research paper on Charles Van den Born's first
aeroplane flight in Hong Kong (Sha Tin, 18 March 1911).

**`https://histsyn.github.io/plates/FirstFlight1911/images/<file>`**

| File | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|
| `cm_1911-03-16_site_col.jpg` | *China Mail*, 16 March 1911, printed p. 6 — the aviation column: the Sha Tin site ("nearly one mile long and three quarters of a mile wide"), the Chanute-Farman machine, and the "rights of the air" paragraph | https://hkintexts.histsyn.com/image-page/1710113 |
| `cm_1911-03-20_eyewitness_col.jpg` | *China Mail*, 20 March 1911, printed p. 7 — the eyewitness column: the crowd that waited, "a big share of Oriental patience", the flight, "the bird-like machine darted down with lightning rapidity" | https://hkintexts.histsyn.com/image-page/1710144 |

| `ohk5_p28_machine.jpg` | *Old Hong Kong*, vol. 5, printed p. 27 — the "Colony's Early Aviation" series: the *S.C.M. Kittendrigh | https://hkintexts.histsyn.com/image-page/1588008 |

All four are documentary scans of newspaper and magazine pages from the
hkintexts corpus, reproduced for research and citation with source, date,
printed page and access date (21 October 2026) given in each caption. No
external photograph is used. The three newspaper plates are **column crops**;
the *Old Hong Kong* leaf is a single-column page and is shown whole.

There is **no photograph of the flight itself** in the corpus's scan set: the
*Old Hong Kong* article announces an old photograph of the machine, but the
plate leaf is not among the scanned leaves of vol. 5, and neither the
*Hongkong Daily Press* nor the *Hongkong Telegraph* has an image twin for
1911. No scan has been fabricated to fill the gap.
